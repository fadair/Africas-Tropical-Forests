# Trees-and-Soil-at-Coed-Llennyrch
[The Interaction of Trees and Soil: Within the Celtic rainforest, Coed Llennyrch of North Wales](FADAIR2020_TreeSoil_WalesLlennyrch.pdf)<br>

Ancient Semi-Natural Woodlands (ASNW) are home to more threatened species in the UK than any other habitat, but have been substantially destroyed by anthropogenic impacts since the Neolithic period and are threatened today. Soil development and its subsequent characteristics are dependent upon the biotic and abiotic variables that make up the climate, landscape, geology, and ecosystem, and may be affected by anthropogenic activities. This study focuses on the relationship soil has with its occupying tree community at the ASNW of Coed Llennyrch, and attempts to tease out any significant relationship between the two. Two sites were investigated (A & B), and results suggest that Site B is older than that of Site A. Betula (birch) and Quercus (oak) differentially prefer soil types, but Betula are restricted to areas of a thicker topsoil, while Quercus have less preference. It also appears that Quercus is incrementally modifying soil characteristics through recalcitrant litter, which may be making conditions unsuitable for Betula cohabitation. Based on these findings and relevant literature it is highly probable that Betula are likely undergoing succession by Quercus at Site A.<br>
